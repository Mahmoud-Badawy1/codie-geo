# Codie GEO editorial playbook: persuasive, human-centered content

## What good means
Write like a skilled editor and domain-aware copywriter: make a real point, give the reader a reason to care, show evidence, then offer a useful next step. Avoid generic AI-sounding prose without pretending the work was not AI-assisted. Do not claim AI-detector evasion; detection tools are unreliable and originality, accuracy and audience usefulness matter more.

## Editorial workflow (required for every article, post and campaign)
1. **Gather the human truth.** Record the specific audience, situation, genuine friction, objection, desired result, brand voice, and trustworthy proof. Ask for genuine anecdotes, customer language, interview snippets or case data only when available. If absent, use a labeled hypothetical situation or a straightforward explanatory opening. Never invent a first-person experience or represent fictional quotes as real.
2. **Choose one sharp idea.** What can a reader learn here that they would not get from five near-identical competitor articles? Identify the useful tension, trade-off, correction, firsthand evidence or practical procedure.
3. **Choose a real opening.** Start with an observable moment, an accurate surprising fact, a costly mistake, an identifiable question, or an immediate result. Make the first two lines specific. Reject canned openers such as 'In today's fast-paced world', 'Unlock the power', 'Game changer', 'Dive into', and 'In an ever-evolving landscape'.
4. **Build story through causality, not formulas.** Situation → friction → stakes → concrete discovery → useful lesson → possible next step is a flexible editing lens, not a visible template. Vary narrative architecture. Don't force every story into AIDA, PAS, or another named marketing formula. A news explainer or technical tutorial may need no personal story.
5. **Earn the next paragraph.** Mix sentence lengths, include concrete nouns and verbs, allow quiet transitions, show examples before abstract generalizations, cut introductions that postpone substance, and remove paragraph-level duplication. Each section should advance the reader's understanding.
6. **Respect the reader.** Empathy means naming legitimate frustrations without shaming, exploiting fears, overdiagnosing feelings, or claiming '90% of people feel this'. For emotional sectors, choose sensitive and culturally appropriate language.
7. **Demonstrate claims.** Cite exact primary sources and dates, distinguish verified evidence from interpretation, quantify only with sourced numbers, and make comparisons fair. No manufactured urgency, scarcity, fabricated product capability, unverifiable superlatives or invented reviews.
8. **Make the action proportionate.** Educational article: optional next step or question. Consideration-stage page: inspect a checklist or product docs. Conversion page: honest product invitation. Community: facilitate discussion before links. No hard sell when a user sought information.
9. **Edit aloud.** Remove filler and repetitive contrast patterns ('not just X, but Y'), hollow three-item lists, excessive rhetorical questions, faux dramatic micro-sentences, unnecessary emojis, mechanical end-of-section summaries, and keyword repetition. Avoid predictable AI-marker flourishes; do not optimize for evasion.
10. **Localization is rewriting.** Arabic may be Egyptian colloquial, Modern Standard Arabic, or another dialect only when profile requests it. Preserve accuracy, register and idiom; avoid literal English sentence structure. Never insert Arabic tatweel U+0640 or decorative dash strips.

## Storytelling options (choose only if it improves understanding)
- 'The point of friction': one true or clearly hypothetical moment that shows the reader's problem.
- 'Before and after a decision': a transparent example of trade-offs, not a fabricated transformation claim.
- 'The false assumption': open with a commonly believed but supportably incomplete idea; replace it with nuance.
- 'A brief field note': a documented issue, verified product release, disclosed incident, or published research finding.
- 'Small concrete detail': an artifact, error message, workflow step, screenshot description or customer quote **only if verified/authorized**.

## Copy mode from the uploaded DOCX (optional; do not force on every post)
- `/codie-geo copy`: create a **long copy** with meaningful depth (600+ words if suitable) and a **short copy** (80+ words if suitable), plus up to **10 distinct visual headlines** and **10 distinct ad hooks**. These lengths came from the supplied example about marketing a book. Use them for a dedicated campaign/copy request, not for X posts or unrelated channels.
- Speak to real pain, the specific situation, believable improvement, and the actual product or next step. Do not imply a book or Life Extraction File exists for every project; those were example-specific inputs.
- The prompt requests avoiding formula frameworks such as AIDA/PAS, no fabrication, repetition or padding. Respect those principles by default; follow user-approved brand voice and audience register.
- A hook must tell the truth; it should invite reading, not coerce with misleading shock or 'you won't believe' claims.

## Revision check: the six questions
1. What does the reader now know or feel able to do?
2. Which sentence contains a concrete, original insight?
3. Which claim would be embarrassing without a source?
4. Did we add scenes, personas, quotes or statistics that don't exist?
5. Could a competent competitor publish this unchanged? If yes, add differentiating evidence.
6. Is the language clean, nonrepetitive and naturally punctuated (especially no U+0640)?
